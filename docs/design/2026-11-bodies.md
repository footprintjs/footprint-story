# Bodies — parts, poses, solvers and the moves a body makes

Status: design, 2026-11-22. Nothing built. It follows `docs/design/2026-11-human-motion.md` ("Bodies (later rounds)")
and keeps its five rules. Lip sync, idle life (breath, blinks) and stairs are later, outside this page.

## Why

`footprint-story/human` moves a point well: a hand reaches by minimum jerk, a ball flies, a chain hangs. A person is
many points held together. An author who wants "she walks to the desk and reaches for the cup" today has to key every
joint by hand, and it comes out stiff, sliding or off balance, differently each try. The same rule as before applies:
the author writes the intent (`{do: 'walk', body: 'ana', to}`), footprint-story decides the steps, the poses and the
timing from laws, and the critics say in words what still looks wrong. The tools only draw.

## Rules this design keeps

1. **The author writes intent; the tool decides the rest.** No joint angle in an entry, ever. An LLM writes one line
   per move, not keyframes.
2. **Same input, same output.** Pure, fixed-step, seeded from the body's name and the entry (never `who`).
3. **Composed, not inherited.** A body is data: a rig, a pose, a look. A dog or a robot is another rig with the same
   solvers, never a subclass.
4. **Named strategies behind one interface.** A body move is a model `{family, name, plan, sample, checks}` like any
   other, chosen by the same rule table, wrapped by the same wrappers, checked by the same critics.
5. **Numbers in footprint-story, drawing in the tools.** footprint-story gives poses and part shapes as plain data;
   StoryReel paints a canvas, StoryDeck writes SVG.
6. **Every refusal and finding names the entry and the fix** in the entry's own keys.

## 1. A body is parts

### The rig (data)

A rig is a tree of parts. Each part owns the joint at its start, so there is no separate joint list: a tree has one
joint per part below the root, and two lists would have to agree.

```js
{ name: 'ana', plane: 'side', facing: 1,            // facing: +1 looks along +x, −1 along −x
  root: 'pelvis',
  parts: [                                           // parents before children: a cycle cannot be written
    { name: 'pelvis',      parent: null,     length: 0,  width: 34, layer: 'mid',  mass: 0.142 },
    { name: 'torso',       parent: 'pelvis', length: 101, rest: -1.571, limit: [-1.9, -1.1], width: 40, layer: 'mid', mass: 0.355 },
    { name: 'upper-arm-n', parent: 'torso',  length: 65, rest: 3.1, limit: [0.5, 4.4], width: 14, layer: 'near', mass: 0.028 },
    { name: 'forearm-n',   parent: 'upper-arm-n', length: 51, rest: 0.2, limit: [0, 2.6], width: 12, layer: 'near', mass: 0.016 },
    …
  ],                                                 // (numbers illustrative)
  chains: [
    { name: 'arm-n', parts: ['upper-arm-n', 'forearm-n'], end: 'hand-n', solver: 'two-bone', bend: 'back' },
    { name: 'leg-n', parts: ['thigh-n', 'shin-n'],         end: 'foot-n', solver: 'two-bone', bend: 'front' },
    { name: 'spine', parts: ['torso', 'neck'],              end: 'head',   solver: 'fabrik' },
  ],
  feet: ['foot-n', 'foot-f'], loose: [] }            // loose: parts that follow through (hair, a scarf, a tail)
```

- `rest` and `limit` are angles relative to the parent, in radians, y down (the human door's frame). `length`,
  `width` in px. `mass` is a share; the shares are scaled to sum to 1.
- `layer` is `near`, `mid` or `far`: which side of the body a part is on, seen side on. It sets paint order (§7)
  and which parts may touch (the self-intersection critic, §6).
- `humanRig({name, height, build})` makes the usual rig from a height in px: segment lengths as shares of height
  (Drillis & Contini, as Winter tabulates them: upper arm 0.186, forearm 0.146, thigh 0.245, shin 0.246, hip height
  0.530) and mass shares (Dempster, via Winter). At 350 px (1.75 m at the package's 200 px a metre) the leg is about
  186 px. A tool's own rig is handed in as data and read like any input (§10).

### The pose (data)

```js
{ root: [x, y], turn: 0, angles: [0, -1.571, 3.1, 0.2, …] }   // angles in the rig's part order
```

Angles are a list in part order, not an object keyed by name: no key can reach a prototype, and the list is as long
as the rig. `placeParts(rig, pose)` (forward kinematics) gives each part's start, end and world angle; `centreOf(rig,
placed)` the centre of mass. Both pure.

### Planar first

Round 1 to 4 rigs are planar (`plane: 'side'`): one upright plane, near and far parts layered in it. WHY: both tools
already plan side on. A 2D figure (the whiteboard's stick figure) is this plane as drawn. An isometric piece is planned
in the upright plane through its way, in the world's px, and laid back on the floor along the way, exactly as StoryReel
0.44 and StoryDeck 0.37 lay a roll or a slide (`footprint-story/isometric` · `sideFoot`, `worldPx`). The body's foot is
the piece's foot (`footOf`); its box at each sample feeds the tools' own `contact` and `covers` critics unchanged. A 3D
rig (quaternion joints, swing–twist limits, from `footprint-story/geometry`) is a later round; the data shape leaves room
(`plane: 'world'`).

## 2. Two drives: voluntary and passive

| drive | the centre of mass | the joints | used for |
|---|---|---|---|
| `voluntary` | planned: over the support, or carried by the footsteps | the human laws on the ends (hand, foot, head), then IK fills the joints | walk, reach, point, look, wave, sit, stand; a jump's crouch and landing |
| `flight` | ballistic in closed form (`physics/flight.js`) | min-jerk to a tuck or a reach pose; loose parts follow through | a jump's time in the air |
| `passive` | not planned: it comes out of the ragdoll | a ragdoll: Verlet points held by part lengths and joint limits | fall, a knock |

The drive is decided by rules as data, `DRIVE_RULES`, in the shape of footprintjs `decide()` rules (first match, the
label is the reason), read by the `choose` step beside `CHOOSE_RULES`:

| rule | then |
|---|---|
| `{when: {do: {in: ['fall']}}}` | `passive` — "a fall: nothing holds the body up" |
| `{when: {do: {eq: 'jump'}}}` | `phases` — "a jump: voluntary crouch, flight, voluntary landing" |
| otherwise | `voluntary` — "the body moves itself" |

It is written per intent under `drive-<i>` (`{drive, phases, rule}`), so `log --why drive-3` says why a body fell
limp. A voluntary move that ends off balance is never turned into a fall by the tool: the balance critic says so and
names the fix. WHY: a fall is a story beat; only the author adds one.

## 3. Solvers

All in one folder, pure, each ending in the one clamp (`clampPose`). Each says what it guarantees and reports, never
hides, what it could not do.

| solver | for | how | guarantees | reports |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `twoBone` | arm, leg | law of cosines: with reach d clamped into [\|l1 − l2\|, l1 + l2], the elbow's inner angle is acos((l1² + l2² − d²) / 2 l1 l2) and the shoulder's offset from the line to the target acos((l1² + d² − l2²) / 2 l1 d); cosines clamped to [−1, 1] | the end on the target exactly when it is in reach; lengths exact; no iteration; the same bend side always | `miss` (px), `stretched` (the target was past reach), `folded` (inside \|l1 − l2\|) |
| `fabrik` | spine, neck, tail, any chain of 3 to 16 | Aristidou & Lasenby's forward and backward passes, each point set back to its part's length; joint limits applied in each pass by turning the child about its joint (Aristidou, Chrysanthou & Lasenby 2016) | lengths exact after every pass; at most 16 passes in a fixed order, so the same answer every time; stops early under 0.1 px | `miss`, `passes`, whether a limit bound |
| `clampPose` | every pose any solver or model makes | each relative angle clamped into its `limit` | every angle within its limit | which parts were clamped and by how much; the end's move from it |

- **The bend rule.** In the plane a two-bone solve has two answers, mirror images. The chain's `bend` (`front` or
  `back`, read against `facing`) picks one: knees bend forward, elbows back. WHY: the answer must never flip between
  frames, and a pole point (the 3D form) is not needed in a plane.
- **Degenerate targets.** A target on the shoulder has no direction: the upper part keeps its previous world angle.
  A target exactly at full reach is straight. Both are rules, so the result never depends on rounding.
- **Lean.** A reach out of the arm's range by no more than the spine can bend first leans the spine (FABRIK on the
  spine chain towards the target), then solves the arm. Beyond that, the reach stays short and the critic says so.
- **Why FABRIK, not cyclic coordinate descent:** it works on positions, not angles, keeps lengths exactly every pass,
  converges in few passes and gives natural spine curves; CCD tends to curl the last joints first.

## 4. Walking

`{do: 'walk', body: 'ana', to: [900, 600]}` or `path: [[…], …]`; optional `speed` (px a second) or `pace`
(`'stroll'`, `'walk'`, `'brisk'`).

**From the leg to the step.** L is the hip height (thigh + shin + the foot's height). g is the package's 1960 px/s².

| number | rule | at L = 186 px, Fr 0.2 |
|---|---|---|
| speed | v = √(Fr · g · L), the Froude number Fr from `pace` (stroll 0.1, walk 0.2, brisk 0.35) | 270 px/s (1.35 m/s) |
| stride (two steps) | λ = 2.3 · L · Fr^0.3 (Alexander 1976) | 263 px |
| step | λ / 2, then evened: n = ⌈distance / step⌉ steps of distance / n, so the last lands on `to` | 132 px |
| cadence | v / step | 2.05 steps a second (123 a minute) |
| contact | stance 60%, swing 40% of a cycle; both feet down for the first and last 10% of each stance (Perry & Burnfield) | — |

A walk asked past Fr 0.5 (the walk–run change, Alexander 1984) is refused, naming the fastest walk; `run` is not in
this design.

**The plan.** `planSteps(rig, path, gait)` gives footsteps: `{foot, at: [x, y], from, to}` (when it is down), feet
alternating, the first a half step from standing, the last bringing the feet together. Then, per sample:

1. The hip rides an arc over the stance foot, as an inverted pendulum (Cavagna, Heglund & Taylor 1977), its height
   set so the stance knee stays bent a little (`KNEE_SOFT`, ours).
2. A swinging foot travels to its next footstep by minimum jerk, lifted on a bow that peaks mid-swing (`LIFT`, ours).
3. Each leg is solved by `twoBone` from the hip to its foot. A planted foot is the footstep's point, so it holds.
4. The arms swing opposite to the legs (`ARM_SWING`, ours); the torso keeps a small lean into the walk.

**Turning.** In the plane, a turn round is one step in place that flips `facing`. In an isometric world each straight
stretch of the way is one side-on walk; a corner is a turn step pivoting on the stance foot, at most 90° a step
(ours). The tools split a walk at each turn into moves (§7). Stairs are later: footsteps on heights, the same planner.

**Critics.**
- `foot-slide`: the most a foot moves while it is down, per contact. Limit 1 px (ours). It should never fire on a
  planned walk; it guards a tool's own model and a wrapped walk. Fix: in the walk's keys (`speed`, `pace`).
- `balance`: for a walk, at each heel strike the extrapolated centre of mass (XCoM = x + v / √(g / L), Hof et al.
  2005) must lie behind the new foot. For a still or slow move (stand, sit, reach), the centre of mass over the floor
  must lie within the planted feet, with a margin of 5% of the foot's length (ours). Fix: a wider stance, a shorter
  reach, or a step first (`{do: 'walk', to: …}` before it).

## 5. Jump

`{do: 'jump', body: 'ana', to, apex?}`. Three phases, recorded in `drive-<i>`:

1. **Crouch** (voluntary, the anticipation): the hip lowers by `CROUCH` (0.25 L, ours) by minimum jerk; feet held.
2. **Flight**: take-off speed from the apex, v = √(2 g h) up; the centre of mass follows `physics/flight.js` · `launch`
   from take-off to landing in closed form. The limbs go by minimum jerk to a tuck pose and back to a landing pose
   before touch-down. Loose parts follow through: each is a Verlet chain pinned at its parent (`physics/chain.js`,
   the hang model's), so a scarf trails and settles on its own.
3. **Landing** (voluntary, the absorb): the feet land on their points at the flight's end; the hip sinks by `ABSORB`
   (0.3 L, ours) by minimum jerk and rises to standing. The feet hold from touch-down.

Default apex `0.3 L` (ours). Critics: `spacing` on the centre in flight (the existing critic: it must fall at its
gravity), `contact` (floor and boxes), `foot-slide` and `balance` at the landing.

## 6. Intents a tool can write

An intent that names a `body` is a body's move. Its `do` is read in `BODY_DOS` (a thing's `do` stays in `HUMAN_DOS`).
A body's moves run in list order and each starts from the pose the previous one of that body ended in; the first from
the rig's rest pose standing at `at`.

| do | family | model (the usual) | keys besides `body` | drive | critics |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `walk` | `walk` | `footsteps` | `to` or `path`, `speed` or `pace` | voluntary | foot-slide, balance, joint-limit, contact |
| `reach` | `limb` | `ik-reach`: the hand by minimum jerk, its seconds from Fitts, joints by `twoBone` (lean by `fabrik`) | `to`, `hand` (`near`, `far`), `target`, `seconds` | voluntary | reach-range, joint-limit, balance, jerk, arc |
| `point` | `limb` | `ik-point`: the hand to 0.9 of the arm's reach on the line from the shoulder to `at`; the head looks too | `at`, `hand` | voluntary | joint-limit, arc |
| `look` | `look` | `gaze`: the head turns by minimum jerk, the eyes 0.05 s first (Freedman 2008) | `at`, `seconds` | voluntary | joint-limit |
| `wave` | `limb` | `wave`: a reach to the raised hand, then forearm swings, each a minimum-jerk half swing | `hand`, `times` (3) | voluntary | joint-limit, jerk |
| `sit` | `posture` | `sit`: lean forward, then the hip down to the seat (the phases of Schenkman et al. 1990, reversed) | `on` (a point or a block's top), `seconds` | voluntary | balance, foot-slide, joint-limit |
| `stand` | `posture` | `stand`: lean forward, rise, straighten | `seconds` | voluntary | balance, foot-slide, joint-limit |
| `jump` | `jump` | `leap` | `to`, `apex` | phases | spacing, contact, foot-slide, balance |
| `fall` | `ragdoll` | `ragdoll` | `push` (`[vx, vy]`, px a second), `at` (the part pushed) | passive | contact, stretch, spacing |

**Through `planIntents`.** No new step. `read` reads `body` and checks it names a rig in the settings
(`planIntents(intents, {bodies: [rig, …]})`; bodies are data, so they may also ride in the run's input). `choose` picks
the model by `CHOOSE_RULES` and the drive by `DRIVE_RULES`. `plan` plans each body's moves in order (the start pose is
the one ordering rule). `wrap` wraps the driving track (a hand, a hip, a centre), before IK: an anticipated reach
winds the hand back, and the joints follow. `sample`, `check` and `findings` are unchanged, because a body sample has
the shape every sample has (§7). humanflow's chart builds itself from the same list, so its property test covers
bodies once the generators make body intents.

**The ragdoll** (`fall`). A point at every joint and part end, Verlet on the package's fixed step (1/240 s), each part
a length constraint, each joint limit enforced by turning the child back into its range after each relaxation pass,
the floor and boxes by `physics/collide.js` (Jakobsen 2001, as the hanging chain). It starts from the current pose
with the motion it had (the previous positions are the pose one step earlier) plus `push` at `at`. It ends at rest:
every point under 4 px/s for 0.25 s (ours, the chain's rest speed), at most 10 s, `basis.stoppedBy` saying which.

**Critics new to bodies** (each `{critic, level, message, fix}`, the fix in the entry's keys):

| critic | measures | finds fault when | example fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| `reach-range` | how far the hand ends from `to` | over 1 px (ours) | "walk closer first: {do: 'walk', body: 'ana', to: [610, 600]}" |
| `joint-limit` | the most a solve had to clamp, as the end's move | over 1 px; a note when a limit bound but the end held | "reach lower: to: [x, y]" or "hand: 'far'" |
| `foot-slide` | a foot's travel while down | over 1 px | "slow it: pace: 'stroll'" |
| `balance` | the margin of the centre (or XCoM) over the support | under 0 | as §4 |
| `self-intersection` (lite) | the overlap of two parts' capsules on the same `layer` that are not parent and child | over 2 px (ours) | "hand: 'far'" or "to: …" |

The existing `jerk`, `arc`, `spacing`, `contact` and `stretch` run on the driving track (a hand, the centre, a link),
unchanged. "Lite" means capsules in the plane, one layer at a time: near and far parts pass each other by design.

## 7. What a tool draws

**A body sample** is a sample like any other: `{x, y}` is the centre of mass (so `spacing` and `contact` read it as
they read a ball), `points` the part ends in rig order, `angles` the world angles in rig order, `down` 1 or 0 per foot.
At most 64 parts, so within `MOST_SAMPLE_LIST`.

**Shapes once, places per frame.** `bodyShapes(rig, look)` gives each part's paint items once, in the part's own
frame: a capsule as a `polygon` (each round end 8 points, ours), the head as an `ellipse`, a stick figure's part as a
`stroke` (the hand-sketch pen, for the whiteboard). `bodyFrame(rig, sample, view)` gives, per part, a transform
`[a, b, c, d, e, f]` (rotate and move, never scale) and the paint order. The items are the isometric paint list's own
kinds (`PAINT_ITEMS`), so each tool's one painter draws them; a body adds no item kind.

**Paint order.** Back to front: the far layer, then mid, then near; within a layer, the rig's part order. Which side
is near is decided in footprint-story from the heading and the view, once per move. The order never changes inside a
move. WHY: StoryDeck's SVG has one document order, which CSS cannot animate. A walk that turns is split at each turn
into moves, so the order may change between them. In an isometric world the whole body is one piece in the map's
back-to-front order (`orderAt`, by its foot).

**StoryDeck** writes each part's shape once as SVG and its transform as CSS keyframes at 60 a second, as it does for a
moving block today (rigid parts, so a transform carries them; no animated path data, which Safari lacks). A jump, a
step back, reduced motion and print land at rest, as for blocks. **StoryReel** samples the plan at each frame's time,
asks `bodyFrame`, sets each part's transform on the canvas and paints its items with the kit's painter.

**Budgets** (ours): a rig at most 64 parts; a chain at most 16; a scene at most 8 bodies; a body's paint list at most
3 items a part (within `MOST_PAINT_ITEMS`, 512); a deck move at most 600 keyframes a part (10 s at 60), a longer move
split. A 10 s walk of an 18-part body is 10,800 transforms in a deck page: the budget a test pins.

## 8. The record

| explained (kept whole, asked by key) | derived (summed up) |
|---|---|
| `model-<i>`, `drive-<i>`: the model, the drive, each rule's words | the poses at every sample |
| `duration-<i>`: seconds and basis — for a walk `{legLength, froude, speed, stride, step, steps, cadence, seconds}`, a reach's Fitts basis, a jump's apex and take-off speed, a fall's `stoppedBy` | the ragdoll's points per step |
| `contacts-<i>`: footstep count, the first and last, contact seconds; the whole list under `contacts` (at most 512 steps a walk, ours) | the paint lists and transforms (the tools') |
| solver results inside the basis: `miss`, `clamped` (part names and amounts), `passes` | |
| `findings-<i>`: each critic, level and fix | |

Poses are derived from the rig, the intent and the laws, so the record keeps their summary as 0.33.1 keeps a map's
paint: `{samples, seconds, parts, bounds, points (the centre at SUMMARY_POINTS samples), digest}`, the digest FNV-1a
(`src/seed/seeded.js` · `fnv1a`) over the poses' JSON at 60 a second, keys sorted. Two runs with the same digest drew
the same body. Each per-intent value stays under `WHY_LIMITS.asked` (1000 characters); a test asks for one body move
of 64 and gets it whole.

## 9. Build order

Each round is pull requests in separate folders that builders can take in parallel; every one adds unit tests,
property tests, the refusals of §10, the door check (`test/doors.test.js`) and a README with an example. The body
maths lives in `src/body/` behind a new door, `footprint-story/body` (rig, pose, solvers, shapes: pure, no intents);
its models and critics register in `src/human/` (which imports `src/body/`, never the reverse).

**Round 1 — a body that reaches (footprint-story only).**
- **1A** `src/body/rig.js` (`readRig`, `humanRig`), `pose.js` (`placeParts`, `centreOf`, `restPose`), `limits.js`
  (`clampPose`), `twoBone.js`. Tests: two-bone against the law of cosines in closed form; any reachable target reached
  within 1e-9 of the reach; lengths exact; the bend side never flips over a swept target; every refusal.
- **1B** `src/body/fabrik.js`. Tests: lengths exact after every pass; reachable targets within 0.1 px in 16 passes;
  limits held; the same answer twice; an unreachable target straightens the chain toward it.
- **1C** (after 1A) the `limb` family, models `ik-reach` and `ik-point`, `BODY_DOS`, the `bodies` setting, critics
  `reach-range` and `joint-limit`, the per-intent keys; humanflow's property test given body intents. Done when
  `planIntents([{do: 'reach', body: 'ana', to}], {bodies: [humanRig({name: 'ana', height: 350})]})` plans, samples,
  checks and explains a reach, and an out-of-reach one says "walk closer first".

**Round 2 — walking.** 2A `src/body/steps.js` (`planSteps`: footsteps from a path, gait numbers, contact phases;
pure, testable alone). 2B (after 2A) the `walk` model and critics `foot-slide`, `balance`; `contacts-<i>`. Tests: the
last foot lands on `to`; gait numbers against the table above; a planted foot moves under 1e-9 px; cadence within the
measured 90 to 140 steps a minute over the `pace` words; an off-balance reach found.

**Round 3 — passive and flight.** 3A `src/human/physics/ragdoll.js` on the chain's Verlet (lengths within 5%, limits
held after each pass, no point under the floor, rests or says why not). 3B `DRIVE_RULES`, the `ragdoll` model and
`fall`. 3C (after 3A) `jump`: crouch, flight, landing, loose parts.

**Round 4 — the tools draw a body.** 4A footprint-story: `bodyShapes`, `bodyFrame`, the near-side rule, the
`self-intersection` critic, the pose summary and its record-size test. Then in parallel, each in its own repository:
4B StoryReel (a world's `motion` and a whiteboard figure take `body`; canvas painting; golden pins move only for new
examples) and 4C StoryDeck (`data-move="ana walk to desk"`; SVG keyframes; print and reduced motion at rest).

**Later:** sit, stand, look, wave (round 5, on rounds 1 and 2); 3D rigs; stairs; idle life; lip sync.

## 10. Threat model

Entries come from people and from models, and both make mistakes; a recipe may also be hostile. Every input is read
by its own stored fields (`read.js` · `stored`: a getter is never run), checked, and refused in words that name it.

| input | bound or rule | why |
|---|---|---|
| a rig's parts | 1 to 64; names `BUILT_IN_NAME`, unique; parents listed before children; exactly one root | a tree by construction, read in one pass; `__proto__` and the like fail the name rule |
| a chain | 2 to 16 parts, each the child of the one before | FABRIK's cost is passes × parts |
| lengths, widths | finite, 0 < length ≤ 10,000 px; width ≥ 0 | NaN and Infinity never reach a solver |
| limits, rest | finite, within [−π, π], min ≤ max, rest inside | a clamp that cannot hold is refused at the door, not found later |
| mass shares | finite, ≥ 0, a sum above 0 | the centre of mass must exist |
| targets, paths | finite, within ±1e6 px; a path at most 1024 points and 1e5 px | keeps the float digits; a walk at most 512 steps |
| seconds | the human door's caps (a wrapped move at most 3600 s); a ragdoll at most 10 s | bounded work per intent |
| a list | at most 64 intents (`MOST_INTENTS`), 8 bodies | bounded work and record per run |
| a tool's own model or rig | under its prefix (`reel:…`), checked once like any | the record names it and keeps no code |

Solvers never loop on data: two-bone is closed form, FABRIK and the ragdoll run fixed counts. A degenerate case
(zero reach, a straight limb, three points in a line) is a named rule, never a division by zero. Outputs are frozen
plain data.

## 11. Open questions for the owner

1. **Planar first.** Rounds 1 to 4 plan bodies side on, as rolls are planned today; an isometric body cannot point or
   look out of its plane until the 3D round. Accept, or build 3D rigs in round 1?
2. **A new door, `footprint-story/body`,** for the rig, solvers and shapes, or everything on `footprint-story/human`?
3. **Off balance.** This page says a finding, never an automatic step or fall. Should the tool instead add a
   corrective step when a reach leaves the support?
